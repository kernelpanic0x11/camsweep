THE IDEA

Start with the devices you can see.

CamSweep discovers IPv4 devices on your attached network, looks up manufacturers, and checks selected TCP ports for camera clues. Use your computer's normal WiFi or Ethernet connection.

A clean scan cannot tell you a room is camera-free. A flag is a reason to identify a device, not proof that someone is recording you.

01 / FIRST SWEEP

Try it on your own network.

  1. Use a network you own or have permission to assess. A hotel or Airbnb WiFi password is not permission to scan. CamSweep actively sends ping and TCP probes.
  2. Install Python 3.10 or newer on a computer. This kit runs from a terminal, not inside Instagram or a phone browser.
  3. Download and extract the ZIP. Open Terminal or PowerShell in the extracted folder. Keep camsweep.py there.
  4. Check Python, then start. The first command prints your version. The second scans your attached LAN.

macOS / Linux

python3 --version
python3 camsweep.py --hide-mac

Windows PowerShell

py -3 --version
py -3 camsweep.py --hide-mac

The header shows the interface and subnet selected from your default route. Confirm they match the network you intend to check. Press Ctrl + C to stop if they do not. A VPN can change this selection.

Let the scan finish. It prints responding and cached entries, manufacturers where known, selected open ports, and camera-clue counts. Runtime depends on your network. Our October 1 macOS run took 23.6 seconds; that is an observation, not a speed guarantee.

Platform check: macOS was live-tested. Windows and Linux network parsing have fixture coverage, but were not live-tested in that session.

02 / INTERPRETATION

A clue needs a second look.

ResultWhat it meansWhat to do
RespondingResponse evidence, a newly resolved neighbor entry, or this computer.Identify the device. Responding does not mean it is a camera.
Cached / unconfirmedA neighbor-cache record without current response confirmation.It may be asleep, offline, or filtering probes. Do not count it as confirmed online.
CAMERA CLUEA camera-brand match or an open port commonly used for streaming or DVRs.Check the physical device and your own router inventory. A hub can match too.
CHECKA smart-home maker or a shared-use port.Investigate before naming it. Amazon also makes devices other than cameras.
No camera clueNo match among this scanner's selected checks.This does not rule out a camera.
Unknown vendorNo maker match in the available lookup data.Use your router inventory or the device's settings; unknown does not mean suspicious.

The scanner uses a bundled manufacturer list and an existing nmap vendor database if available. It does not send a vendor lookup to an external service. Randomized MAC addresses can obscure the maker. An open port establishes a TCP connection, not what is actually running behind it.

03 / OUR ACTUAL TEST

It found the hub. The camera slept.

On October 1, 2026, Grant tested his own Blink setup on his home network. A Blink-brand device was flagged even though the checked camera ports were closed. Grant identified that device as the Sync Module.

A battery camera did not answer pings during the quiet baseline. When Grant opened Live View in the official Blink app, it began answering. The scanner listed its maker as Amazon Technologies and marked it for checking; it had no open camera ports among those tested. It stopped answering shortly after Live View closed.

A later revised-scanner run showed 10 responding entries and 5 cached/unconfirmed entries. The battery camera was cached/unconfirmed in that run. Its saved name was a prior manual identification, not a new camera detection.

About the reel: the TV/laptop/camera visuals illustrate a possible result. The recorded evidence is the home Blink test above; it does not establish a camera discovery at an Airbnb. Your kit excludes Grant's private device labels and raw scan reports.

04 / BLIND SPOTS

Network visibility has boundaries.

  • Different network: cameras on a separate WiFi, VLAN, or cellular connection are outside this scan.
  • No network: a camera recording only to a memory card cannot appear.
  • Sleeping devices: battery cameras may be quiet or absent during a sweep.
  • Client isolation: guest networks can prevent connected devices from seeing each other.
  • IPv6 and filtering: this is an IPv4 inventory with selected probes, not an exhaustive service audit.

CamSweep does not locate a lens in a room, intercept radio traffic, bypass passwords, or display a video feed. Pair it with a visual check of objects and openings facing private areas. Do not take apart unfamiliar electrical fixtures.

05 / FOLLOW UP

Turn a flag into an identification.

  1. Compare against your own inventory. Check your router's device list and the settings or labels on equipment you own. Names and manufacturer matches are useful leads.
  2. Test a known device. For your own battery camera, open Live View in its official app and rescan while it stays open. A change can help identify it; no change is inconclusive.
  3. Keep notes. Record what you physically identified and what remains uncertain. Do not treat a manufacturer guess as a confirmed device type.
  4. If you find an unfamiliar recording device in a private area, leave the area if you feel unsafe, document its location without handling it, and contact the property/platform support. A scan flag alone is not evidence of a hidden recording device.

Optional: save a private report

python3 camsweep.py --json report.json

On Windows, replace python3 with py -3. JSON contains IP and MAC addresses. --hide-mac hides MAC addresses only in terminal output; IPs remain visible and JSON retains MACs. Redact before sharing.

06 / TROUBLESHOOTING

When the first run is confusing.

“Python not found” or an old version

Install Python 3.10+ from the official Python distribution for your platform, reopen your terminal, and check the version again.

“Can't open file”

Extract the ZIP first and open your terminal in the folder containing camsweep.py. Confirm with ls on macOS/Linux or dir in PowerShell.

Only my laptop or router appears

Check for guest isolation, a VPN, or sleeping devices. Compare with your own router inventory. Do not disable a firewall or assume the room is clear.

The network is too large

The script rejects networks larger than /20. On a network you administer, select an authorized smaller range inside the detected LAN with --subnet. It will reject ranges outside the attached LAN.

A known device is intermittent

Try python3 camsweep.py --timeout 0.6. A longer timeout may help slow responses; it cannot overcome isolation or make an offline device visible.

A TV or computer is flagged

Ports and manufacturer prefixes are shared. Identify the device before drawing conclusions. CamSweep does not authenticate the service as a camera.
